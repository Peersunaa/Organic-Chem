# Reaction Builder: pick a substrate, pick a reagent, see what forms and why

Date: 2026-10-21. Source: Peera's request ("click the reactant like toluene + reagent and get one, there will be a lot", "not just ring but alkene reaction too") and the professor's handwritten EAS notes (`study-guide/prof/eas-notes.pdf`, 11 scanned pages, untracked; page renders `study-guide/prof/pNN.png`).

## What it is

Route `#build` ("Reaction builder"). Two pickers and a result:

1. **Substrate picker**: drawn tiles, grouped: Alkenes (ethylene, propene, 2-methylpropene, but-1-ene, cis/trans-but-2-ene, 2-methylbut-2-ene, cyclohexene, 1-methylcyclohexene, 1,2-dimethylcyclohexene, styrene, methylenecyclohexane), Alkynes (ethyne, propyne, but-1-yne, but-2-yne, hex-1-yne, hex-3-yne, phenylacetylene), Rings (benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, tert-butylbenzene, anisole, phenol, aniline, acetanilide, chlorobenzene, bromobenzene, fluorobenzene, nitrobenzene, benzaldehyde, acetophenone, benzoic acid, benzenesulfonic acid, p-xylene, p-bromotoluene, p-chlorotoluene, m-nitrotoluene, p-methoxyphenol? (only if the engine can direct it), 4-tert-butyltoluene, p-toluenesulfonic acid). Every tile is a SMILES the engine accepts; the builder also lets the learner type a SMILES or a name (course namer) as a custom substrate, validated.
2. **Reagent picker**: tiles grouped as the professor numbers them for rings, then alkene and alkyne groups: Ring: 1 Halogenation (Br₂/FeBr₃, Cl₂/AlCl₃, Cl₂/CH₃COOH for activated rings, I₂/HNO₃), 2 Protonation/deuteration (D₂SO₄/D₂O; H⁺ ipso removal of t-butyl and SO₃H), 3 Nitration (HNO₃/H₂SO₄), 4 Friedel–Crafts acylation (RCOCl/AlCl₃ then H₃O⁺ workup; (RCO)₂O), 5 Friedel–Crafts alkylation (RCl/AlCl₃ or FeCl₃ with 3°, 2°, 1° halides; alkene + HF/BF₃; alcohol + BF₃), 6 Sulfonation (SO₃/H₂SO₄) and desulfonation (dilute H₂SO₄, heat), plus reductions the course has (Clemmensen Zn(Hg)/HCl, Wolff–Kishner H₂NNH₂/KOH), side chain (KMnO₄ hot; NBS/hν). Alkene: HBr, HCl, H₂O/H⁺, Br₂, Cl₂, H₂/Pd, cold KMnO₄, hot KMnO₄, RCO₃H, RCO₃H then H₃O⁺, BH₃ then H₂O₂/NaOH. Alkyne: HX (1 eq, 2 eq), X₂ (1, 2 eq), H₂O/Hg²⁺, H₂/Lindlar, Na/NH₃, H₂/Pd, NaNH₂ then RBr, BH₃ then H₂O₂.
3. **Result panel**: the product(s) drawn (major and minor with the professor's ratios where he gives them: ortho/para split for toluene nitration-like cases, "1 isomer" for meta directors, "horrible mixture" cases flagged), the three facts (what adds, where, how in space) or for rings (which position, why: class of the director, the professor's hierarchy), the mechanism (scene when one exists, else the step player with the engine's mechanism, else a drawn scheme from the hub card), a one-paragraph "why" in beginner voice citing the professor's rule by name, an energy-diagram sketch for o/p vs meta σ-complexes when a ring reacts, and "No reaction / not useful" cards with the reason when the pair does not work (FC on a meta-directing ring; FC on aniline (N coordinates AlCl₃); 1° halide alkylation rearranges, show the rearranged product; polyalkylation warning; alcohol alkylation limited).
4. **Chain**: a "Use this product next" button puts the product in the substrate slot so multi-step sequences can be built, including the professor's blocking-group sequences (toluene → t-butyl para → Br₂ ortho → ipso H⁺ removes t-butyl; toluene → SO₃H para → HNO₃ ortho → H⁺ removes SO₃H; FC acylation then Clemmensen to a straight chain). A breadcrumb shows the sequence; "copy as text" gives the route.
5. **Coverage counter**: "N substrate × reagent pairs available" computed from the engines; a "random pair" button; "quiz me" flips to predict mode (hide the product, show four options from the existing generators) using the reaction-modes machinery.

## Chemistry rules to encode (professor's notes, pages 7–11), on top of the course engines

- Director classes: (1) strongly activating o/p: –OH, –OR, –NH₂, –NR₂, –NHCOR, –OCOR; (2) alkyl and halogens: alkyl/aryl o/p activating, halogens o/p deactivating; (3) meta directors, deactivating: –NO₂, –SO₃H, –COOH, –COR, –CHO, –CN, –CO₂R.
- Two substituents, different classes: the superior class decides (NH₂, OH, NR₂ > OR, OCOR, NHCOR > R, Ar > halogens > meta directors). All o/p directors dominate over meta directors.
- Two substituents, same class: mixtures; show all positions with "mixture" and the professor's example ratios where available (o-chlorotoluene 21/19/42/17, 3,4-dichloro... as in page 9).
- Substitution at the carbon between two substituents meta to each other: low (show as minor, "E⁺ unlikely to enter between two groups").
- Ortho effect: when a meta director is meta to an o/p director, the incoming E⁺ goes ortho to the meta-directing group rather than para to it (p-bromotoluene nitration → 2,3-dinitro-4-bromotoluene example on page 9: encode the rule, show the professor's example).
- Meta-directing rings give one isomer in high yield (benzaldehyde nitration 75–84%, nitrobenzene bromination 60–75%) but do NOT undergo FC acylation/alkylation.
- Bulky acylation agents give mainly para with o/p directors (toluene + PhCOCl: 9% ortho, 1% meta, 90% para); recrystallisation isolates para.
- FC alkylation: 3° and 2° halides work (also alkenes with HF/BF₃, protonation to the same cation); 1° halides rearrange via hydride shift (1-chloropropane → isopropylbenzene); polyalkylation because the product is more activated; acylation preferred, then Clemmensen or Wolff–Kishner to the alkyl chain.
- Deuteration/protonation: D₂SO₄/D₂O exchanges ring H (excess → C₆D₆); H⁺ ipso-substitution removes t-butyl (→ isobutylene) and SO₃H (→ SO₂): blocking groups.
- Iodination needs I₂ + HNO₃ (I⁺ via oxidation). Activated rings halogenate under milder conditions (Cl₂/CH₃COOH on p-xylene).
- Sulfonation is reversible (2 H₂SO₄ ⇌ H₃O⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + SO₃).
